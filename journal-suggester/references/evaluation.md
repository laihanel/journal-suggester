# Screening and comparison

## Scope is a gate

Assess each dimension from explicit evidence:

| Dimension | Evidence to inspect | Common mismatch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | Aims and scope; recent ordinary issues | Shared keyword but different research question |
| Audience | Stated readership; related papers | Method specialists versus application readers |
| Method | Author instructions; comparable studies | Venue excludes the study design |
| Article type | Accepted formats and restrictions | Unsolicited reviews or case reports not accepted |
| Contribution | Stated editorial criteria | Requires methodological novelty; manuscript only applies an existing method |
| Context | Population, scale, geography, application | Local case study where broad validation is explicitly required |

Use `pass`, `fail`, or `unresolved` for scope. A confirmed exclusion fails even with high metrics, free OA, or a suggester match. Interpret novelty expectations as a possible mismatch, not an acceptance prediction. If official scope is unavailable, alternate evidence can justify a provisional candidate, not a verified pass.

Related-paper evidence should include DOI/link, year, what was examined (title, abstract, or full text), and the specific overlap and difference. Title-only matches are weaker evidence. Treat special-issue articles, invited work, editorials, and older policies cautiously. Several papers from one guest-edited issue are not independent evidence of the regular journal's policy. Lack of Crossref results is inconclusive because metadata and abstracts are incomplete.

Do not rank by raw number of retrieved papers, citation totals, publisher size, or a single retrieval score. Deduplicate articles by DOI and journals by ISSN/eISSN with verified title relationships; preserve title changes. Search multiple query formulations for interdisciplinary work and avoid letting the largest contributing field dominate automatically.

## Requirements and ranking

Apply user-specified hard constraints before ranking: explicit indexing collections, APC budget, allowed OA license/embargo, article type, language, and exclusions. Mark unknown compliance unresolved. If a user requires a guaranteed decision or acceptance by a date, explain that historical timing cannot establish that guarantee; do not label a journal deadline-safe from a median.

Keep separate judgments for scope, audience, methods/article type, visibility/metrics, timing, cost, and evidence confidence. Qualitative `strong / moderate / weak / unknown` judgments with reasons are sufficient. Numerical scores are optional only when requested; explain the rubric, keep missing values distinct from zero, and show whether different reasonable priorities change the order.

## Metrics and indexing

- JIF, five-year JIF, JCI, CiteScore, SJR, and SNIP are distinct measures. Record the metric name, data year, source, and category/rank when relevant. Distinguish JIF data year from JCR release year.
- Report every relevant verified category or explicitly name the category selected. Do not report an unlabeled best quartile. JCR and SJR quartiles are not interchangeable; neither is a CAS classification.
- Verify the exact collection required (e.g. SCIE, SSCI, ESCI, Scopus, MEDLINE). A JIF does not establish SCIE/SSCI membership. PubMed, MEDLINE, and PMC mean different things.
- Record coverage dates, discontinued coverage, and title/ISSN matches. An article indexed in the past does not prove a journal is currently covered.
- Do not use journal-level metrics to estimate the manuscript's quality or likelihood of acceptance. Avoid comparing raw impact numbers across unrelated fields as if they had equal meaning.
- If authoritative access is unavailable, publisher-reported metrics may be attributed as such; distinguish these from direct index verification.

## Timing

Record the endpoint, units, median/mean, population, reporting period, and source:

1. Submission to first editorial decision (may include desk rejections).
2. Submission to first decision after external review, when separately reported.
3. Submission to acceptance (usually based on accepted papers).
4. Acceptance to online publication.

Never relabel these as equivalent "review time." Preserve the publisher's definition. If population or period is missing, say so. Do not infer timing from Crossref created/deposited/indexed dates. Article-history dates can describe a labeled sample of accepted articles, but cannot reveal rejected submissions or establish journal-wide turnaround. User reports are anecdotal supplementary evidence.

## Integrity and submission policies

Check journal identity and official domain, editorial governance, review process, fee transparency, and any credible current indexing/editorial notices relevant to the choice. Avoid categorical blacklists based solely on publisher name or geography. Report specific sourced concerns. A single retracted paper does not establish a journal-wide problem.

Check decision-relevant preprint/prior-publication rules, word limits, data/code policies, ethics/reporting requirements, and invitation restrictions. Recommend only manuscript-compatible venues or explain the change needed. Do not turn routine selection into an exhaustive integrity audit without a concrete reason.

Before finalizing, check that each preferred option passes scope, that constraints have not disappeared during ranking, that metric categories and timing endpoints are labeled, and that factual citations support the adjacent claims.
